The answer

Renewal Guard

Contract & license auto-renewal deadline tracker — built and published, running daily.

Live and running Zapier Automation, scoped around a real back-office automation role in a famous Ad & Media Company.

Back-office teams manage dozens of vendor contracts, SaaS licenses, insurance policies, and staffing agreements — many with auto-renewal clauses that require cancellation notice by a specific date, or the contract silently renews for another term. Tracking this usually means a spreadsheet nobody consistently checks. I built Renewal Guard: a daily automation that calculates each contract's real cancellation deadline, drafts a tone-matched AI reminder as that date approaches, and sends it to the real owner — live, not a mockup.

The problem

Auto-renewal is the default, not the exception

Most vendor contracts, SaaS licenses, and insurance policies auto-renew unless someone actively cancels within a notice window — commonly 30 to 90 days before the renewal date.

Ownership is scattered, tracking is manual

A given company's contracts span legal, HR, accounting, and IT — each function tracks its own, usually in a spreadsheet nobody's on the hook to check daily. The omission is silent until the renewal charge shows up.

Built with

Google Sheets as the system of record, orchestrated by Zapier (schedule trigger, loop, filter), with Claude drafting the reminder copy and Gmail delivering it — a 6-step automation, published and running daily.

The business case, sized honestly

No measured baseline exists yet — this is a personal build, not a deployed team tool with a usage history. The mechanism itself is what's real; the dollar figure below is an illustrative order of magnitude, not a result.

A missed 30-to-90-day cancellation window doesn't cost a little — it locks the contract into a full extra renewal term. For a back office tracking even 50 real vendor and software agreements averaging $8K–$15K a year, catching just 2–3 renewals that would otherwise have been missed avoids roughly $16K–$45K in unwanted spend annually — illustrative, not measured, but the mechanism (calculate the real deadline, escalate as it closes) is the same regardless of which real numbers eventually go in.

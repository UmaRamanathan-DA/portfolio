Product Case · UX Audit & Teardown

A first-time visitor couldn't tell who the fellowship was for, what it led to, or why the price kept changing.

Independent UX teardown submitted for a hiring competition hosted by ProductSpace (Jul 2025) — a 2-day audit and set of redesign recommendations, not a shipped redesign.

The Product Space PM Fellowship page had real content — curriculum, mentors, placements — but no clear audience fit, no career-outcome framing, and pricing that looked arbitrary even to people in the same cohort. I tore the page apart section by section, mapped 16 issues to specific usability heuristics, and benchmarked it against NextLeap and GrowthX to prioritize what to fix first.

Who Prospective fellowship applicants First-time visitors deciding whether this program is for them, before they'd consider applying.
What Turn an info page into a case for applying Not just present curriculum and pricing — make the case for why someone should trust and choose it.
Why 16 issues, each with a fix Every usability problem mapped to a specific heuristic and a concrete recommendation, not a general note.

Two things that were broken

Same cohort, different prices

Users discovered different discounts within the same cohort with no explanation, damaging trust and perceived fairness.

No way to tell if it's for you

Nothing on the page said who the fellowship was actually for — students, career switchers, or working PMs — or what job it leads to.

What we changed

  • Rewrote the hero around outcome"Build real AI-powered products. Land your next PM job." in place of a vague, unclear tagline.
  • Replaced arbitrary pricing with segmentsStudent, alumni, and early-bird tiers, explained — instead of unexplained same-cohort discounts.
  • Added a career-outcomes sectionTypical roles and an APM → PM → Senior PM pathway, so visitors can see if it fits their goals.
  • Redesigned the CTA funnelStaged CTAs (explore curriculum → see projects → apply) plus a waitlist for closed cohorts, instead of one flat "Enrol Now."

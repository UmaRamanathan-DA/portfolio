The problem

Attrition currently sits at 16.1% — 237 of 1,470 employees left, 1,233 stayed. That number usually shows up after the fact: a quarterly report, or an exit interview. By then the decision to leave has already been made, and replacement cost is already in motion.

The brief was straightforward. Score each employee’s risk of leaving, then attach an intervention that fits the score — a light check-in at the low end, a retention conversation in the middle, an escalation when both risk and value are high. The model flags. A person decides.

The model and how to read it

We trained class-weighted logistic regression and random forest on an 80/20 stratified split, so the 16% attrition rate held in both sets. 294 employees were held out and never seen during training. Logistic regression is the model we recommended: it is explainable, and it is the one that actually catches leavers.

AUC 0.798

Ranks at-risk employees well above chance

Recall 61.7%

Share of actual leavers correctly flagged — the operating metric

Accuracy 75.2%

Lower than a “everyone stays” baseline (84%) — and that is expected

Precision 34.5%

Of those flagged, about 1 in 3 did leave

Accuracy is the wrong score here. Predicting that everyone stays is 84% accurate and catches nobody (0% recall). Random forest looks stronger on accuracy (83.3%) but only recalls 38.3% of leavers. Logistic regression trades some accuracy for 61.7% recall — about 6 in 10 people who actually left. Precision of 34.5% means false alarms; those are cheap next to a missed resignation. The 0.5 threshold is a dial HR can move, not a fixed scientific result.

Four scenarios on the hold-out set

Every one of the 294 held-out employees falls into one of four groups. That is how we briefed HR: not a single accuracy number, but who needs what.

192 Quietly fine Stayed · not flagged

No intervention. Confirms the model is not over-flagging.

55 False alarm Stayed · flagged

A brief, low-key check-in. Cheap even when the flag is wrong.

18 Missed entirely Left · looked low-risk

The blind spot — about 4 in 10 leavers. High-value roles still need a manual safety net.

29 Caught in time Left · flagged

Where the value lives. Retention conversation within days.

The business case

SHRM puts replacement cost at 50–200% of annual salary. Average pay in this dataset is about $57K. Of the 29 leavers the model caught, retaining even one in three — roughly 10 people — avoids about $290K to $1.1M in replacement cost. False alarms are conversations. Missed resignations are replacement cycles. That is why we optimised for recall, not accuracy.

The 84 flagged employees are not treated the same:

Low risk

Quarterly check-in for the HRBP. No urgency; logged for trend tracking.

Elevated risk

Retention conversation within two weeks. The human still makes the call.

High risk, high value

Escalate to manager and HRBP together — the costliest miss to allow.

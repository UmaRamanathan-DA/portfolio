Product Case · Data Analysis & Forecasting

A Glasgow taxi company had trip data — and no clear view of demand patterns, pricing anomalies, or fleet allocation.

This is a Product Strategy Growth problem.

Academic data science project completed with Kin Karta Consulting at the University of Strathclyde Business School, using Haggis Hopper's real historical trip data across Glasgow.

Demand wasn't the mystery — Haggis Hopper already had the trip volume. What was missing was where fleet capacity should sit by hour and postcode, and whether the pricing riders were charged actually held up against the data. I turned raw trip records into live dashboards and a forecasting model, so fleet deployment and pricing could be decided from evidence instead of instinct.

Two things that were invisible

Fleet allocation was a guess

Demand across Glasgow shifts by hour and postcode, but without mapped patterns, vehicles couldn't be positioned where and when riders actually needed them.

Pricing wasn't checked against the data

Fares hadn't been validated against actual trips, leaving the fare-distance relationship and pricing anomalies unexamined.

What we changed

  • Mapped demand by hour and placeTemporal and geospatial patterns surfaced hotspots and under-served postcodes, not just a citywide average.
  • Built live dashboards, not static reportsTableau dashboards let stakeholders monitor demand, revenue, and fleet performance themselves, on demand.
  • Tested whether demand is predictableTime-series and machine learning models forecast short-term demand, moving allocation from reactive to planned.
  • Put pricing under the microscopeA dedicated pricing deep-dive checked tariff logic, fare-distance correlation, and flagged anomalies.

Where demand happens, and whether pricing holds up — now visible, not assumed.
